What we help with

Inflammation is a signal. The question is what keeps turning it on.

When the immune system feels overactive, reactive, or misdirected, the answer is rarely one isolated symptom. Idaho Regenerative Medicine (IRM) looks for terrain patterns that may keep inflammation active: gut health, environmental burden, stealth infections, hormones, nutrients, stress physiology, and tissue injury.

Schedule a VisitBack to What We Help With

The slow fire matters.

Inflammation can be local, systemic, or both. Understanding the difference changes the care conversation.

This may sound like you

The pattern is real, even when it has been hard to name.

  • You have pain, swelling, sensitivity, flares, or symptoms that move around.
  • You have been told markers are normal, but your body still feels inflamed.
  • Food, stress, infections, hormones, toxins, or sleep changes seem to trigger flares.
  • You want help understanding the drivers rather than just suppressing the signal.

You do not need to know which therapy you need before you begin. The first step is helping you feel heard, then deciding what information would create the clearest next step.

The IRM lens

What we may look for underneath the symptom.

Care is individualized. The goal is to understand the pattern, not force your story into a pre-set protocol.

Inflammatory markers

Labs may be useful, but symptoms can appear before markers become abnormal.

Gut and immune tolerance

Digestive health, microbiome balance, and barrier integrity can influence immune reactivity.

Environmental and infectious load

Mold, heavy metals, Lyme, co-infections, chronic viruses, and toxic burden may be considered when clinically relevant.

Your path at IRM

How care typically begins.

Autoimmune and inflammatory patterns often involve immune communication, gut health, toxins, hormones, and nervous system load — not just one lab marker.

  • Get to Know You ConsultMap flares, triggers, prior diagnoses, and what stability or remission would look like for you.
  • Terrain-focused assessmentLabs, gut evaluation, environmental burden, nutrient status, and immune patterns may help prioritize the starting point.
  • Whole-person supportIV therapy, ozone, peptides, hormone context, and lifestyle foundations — sequenced to reduce load and support resilience.

The first step for most patients is a Get to Know You Consult. Learn about becoming a new patient →

Is inflammation always bad?

No. Acute inflammation is part of healing. The concern is when the signal becomes chronic, excessive, or disconnected from resolution.

What if I already have an autoimmune diagnosis?

IRM can look at the broader terrain and support the body while respecting the care team and medical context you already have.
