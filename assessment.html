Assessment

When “normal” is not enough, we build a clearer map.

Assessment at Idaho Regenerative Medicine (IRM) is not about ordering every test possible. It is about listening to the story, identifying the pattern, and choosing the information that may actually change the plan.

Book an AssessmentSee the Framework

Listen & Map

Your symptoms, history, exam findings, prior labs, imaging, and lived experience all matter. Assessment helps organize the pieces so the next step feels informed instead of random.

Why assessment matters

Symptoms can speak before labs or imaging do.

Many patients arrive after being told their results are “normal,” even though their body still feels inflamed, exhausted, painful, stuck, or unable to recover. Normal can be useful information, but it is not the same as optimal function.

At IRM, assessment helps bridge the gap between what has already been checked and what the body may still be communicating. We look for patterns across systems: local tissue injury, systemic inflammation, hormones, metabolism, gut health, immune load, environmental burden, nervous system stress, and the body’s ability to repair.

What we may evaluate

Evaluation chosen by the question, not by a template.

Not every patient needs the same workup. The goal is to identify the missing information that would clarify priorities, change the plan, or help explain why the body is not repairing the way it should.

Imaging

In-office ultrasound assessment

Musculoskeletal ultrasound can help evaluate joints, tendons, ligaments, nerves, fascia, and soft tissues. It can also support precision when ultrasound-guided injection therapies are appropriate.

Blood

Labs & live blood analysis

Bloodwork, live blood analysis, and related markers may help assess inflammation, metabolism, hormones, immune patterns, nutrients, cardiovascular risk, and whole-body resilience.

Terrain

Gut health, drainage & environmental burden

When clinically appropriate, deeper evaluation may include gut health, mold exposure, heavy metals, environmental toxins, lymphatic or detox-related patterns, and elimination pathways that influence repair capacity.

Signals

Hormones, nutrients & metabolism

Hormones, nutrient status, blood sugar, insulin signaling, mitochondrial strain, and metabolic function can shape energy, recovery, body composition, and tissue repair.

Personalization

Genetics, cardiovascular risk & cellular health

Genetic and cellular health evaluation may help personalize decisions around methylation, nutrient needs, cardiovascular risk, inflammation, oxidative stress, and long-term vitality.

Immune load

Lyme, tick-borne infections & co-infections

For patients with persistent, unexplained symptoms, assessment may include Lyme, tick-borne infections, co-infections, chronic viral patterns, and immune stressors that keep the body under strain.

How it fits

Better information should lead to a better sequence.

Assessment is not the destination. It helps us decide what to restore first, where regenerative therapies may fit, and which foundations need support so the body can respond.

Do I need a full workup before I can start care?

Not always. Some patients begin with a consultation, history, exam, and records review. Additional evaluation is considered when it may change the plan or clarify the next best step.

What if my labs or imaging already look normal?

Normal results are still useful, but they do not always explain function. Symptoms may show up before conventional markers change, and the pattern still deserves attention.

Is ultrasound only for injections?

No. In-office ultrasound can help assess musculoskeletal issues and can also be used to guide injection therapies when appropriate.

Will every patient need advanced labs?

No. Advanced labs are selected based on the person, the symptoms, the history, and the clinical question. The goal is clarity, not more data for its own sake.
