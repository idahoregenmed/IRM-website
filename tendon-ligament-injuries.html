What we help with

Sprains, strains, and old injuries can become patterns the body never finished healing.

Tendons and ligaments are more than attachment points. They help coordinate joint stability, movement, proprioception, and repair. When they remain irritated or lax, the body may compensate with muscle spasm, nerve sensitivity, swelling, and recurring pain.

Schedule a VisitBack to What We Help With

A rubber band that lost elasticity.

Dr. Walton often describes ligament laxity like a rubber band that has lost its snap. The joint may still move, but the support system is not doing its full job.

This may sound like you

The pattern is real, even when it has been hard to name.

  • You had a sprain, strain, sports injury, fall, or overuse pattern that never fully resolved.
  • The pain improves temporarily and returns when you increase activity.
  • You feel unstable, tight, guarded, weak, or like the area does not trust itself.
  • You want precise assessment rather than guessing where the tissue damage is.

You do not need to know which therapy you need before you begin. The first step is helping you feel heard, then deciding what information would create the clearest next step.

The Idaho Regenerative Medicine (IRM) lens

What we may look for underneath the symptom.

Care is individualized. The goal is to understand the pattern, not force your story into a pre-set protocol.

Ligament laxity

Small areas of instability can create large compensation patterns.

Tendon irritation

Chronic tendon patterns may need mechanical signaling, biologic support, or rehabilitation sequencing.

Ultrasound-guided precision

In-office ultrasound can help evaluate tissue during movement and guide therapies precisely when appropriate.

Your path at IRM

How care typically begins.

Tendons and ligaments heal slowly because they have limited blood supply. Regenerative care focuses on stability, repair signaling, and the environment around the injury.

  • Get to Know You ConsultReview how the injury happened, what rehab you have done, and whether the tissue still feels unstable or slow to recover.
  • Targeted assessmentOrthopedic exam and ultrasound may help identify the involved structure and whether injection therapies are appropriate.
  • Repair-focused planProlotherapy, PRP, prolozone, shockwave, and rehabilitation support are considered in sequence based on tissue readiness.

The first step for most patients is a Get to Know You Consult. Learn about becoming a new patient →

Why do muscles spasm around an injury?

The brain may use muscle tension to stabilize an area when ligaments are not providing enough support.

Can tendons and ligaments be evaluated with ultrasound?

In many cases, ultrasound can reveal tissue quality, micro-damage, and dynamic movement patterns that are not obvious at rest.
