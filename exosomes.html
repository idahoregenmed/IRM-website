How IRM thinks

How exosomes fit regenerative care.

Exosomes are tiny extracellular vesicles involved in cell-to-cell communication. In regenerative care, they may be considered for their signaling role in tissue repair and inflammatory modulation.

At IRM, exosome-focused approaches may be discussed in orthopedics, tissue repair, or regenerative aesthetics when they fit the assessment and treatment sequence.

The decision depends on the tissue target, the repair environment, the patient's goals, and how exosomes fit alongside other tools such as PRP, stem cell therapies, prolotherapy, shockwave, or whole-body support.

What to expect

For patients considering exosome therapy.

Exosome approaches are discussed as repair-signaling tools — not as guaranteed outcomes. This overview helps you understand the conversation.

Before

Biologics assessment

Your provider clarifies tissue targets, prior care, and whether exosome signaling fits the sequence — often after consult and orthopedic evaluation.

During

Targeted delivery

Depending on the plan, exosome-focused products may be injected into specific tissue or used in aesthetic contexts when clinically appropriate.

After

Individual response

Recovery and activity guidance depend on the delivery site and what else is in your plan. There is no standard one-size-fits-all timeline.

Context

Not a standalone fix

Exosomes work best when inflammation, stability, nutrients, and nervous system tone are also considered.

Are exosomes FDA-approved for my condition?

Exosome products exist in evolving regulatory contexts. Your provider discusses what is being considered, why, and what alternatives exist.

How are exosomes different from PRP?

PRP uses concentrated platelets from your blood. Exosomes are extracellular vesicles focused on cell communication — a different biologic signal with different considerations.

Are results guaranteed?

No. Outcomes vary based on tissue quality, health history, inflammation, adherence to the plan, and individual biology.

Are exosomes stem cells?

No. Exosomes are not stem cells. They are signaling vesicles associated with cell communication.

Where might exosomes be used?

They may be considered across orthopedic tissue repair and regenerative aesthetics when clinically appropriate.

How do I know if this belongs in my plan?

The starting point is the Get to Know You Consult. If IRM may be a fit, the next step may include assessment, orthopedic exam, ultrasound imaging when appropriate, and a personalized care discussion.
