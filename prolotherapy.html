Stability-focused injection therapy

Prolotherapy helps restore stability so the joint can function more like it was designed to.

Prolotherapy is the grandfather of regenerative injection therapy. At Idaho Regenerative Medicine (IRM), it is considered when joint instability and ligament laxity appear to be part of the pain pattern.

Learn About the ConsultRegenerative Orthopedics

A rubber band analogy.

Ligaments are meant to act like strong, responsive rubber bands. When they lose tension, muscles may guard and the joint may become painful.

How IRM thinks

Why stability changes the repair environment.

Prolotherapy uses a dextrose-based solution placed into precise areas of ligament laxity, joint instability, or connective tissue stress to stimulate a localized repair response.

The goal is not simply to numb pain. The goal is to improve stability. When ligaments begin doing their job again, the joint can move with better biomechanics.

With improved stability and function, cartilage, discs, and surrounding connective tissues may have a better environment for repair.

Clinical context

A focused therapy inside a bigger plan.

This therapy is selected within a bigger orthopedic and whole-person plan. The best starting point is understanding the tissue target, the repair environment, and the sequence of care.

Purpose

Support ligament integrity

Prolotherapy is considered when the clinical picture suggests that lax or injured ligaments are contributing to instability and recurring pain.

Biomechanics

Help the joint move better

Improving stability can reduce compensatory muscle guarding and create a better mechanical environment for repair.

Parent category

Part of regenerative orthopedics

Prolotherapy may be used alone or in sequence with prolozone, biologics, nerve-focused therapies, shockwave, or rehabilitation support.

What to expect

For patients considering prolotherapy.

Prolotherapy is a series of targeted injections — not a one-time fix. Here is how patients often experience the process at IRM.

Before

Assessment & planning

A consult and orthopedic exam help confirm ligament laxity or instability is part of your pattern before prolotherapy is scheduled.

During

Targeted injections

A dextrose-based solution is injected into precise areas of ligament or connective tissue stress. Ultrasound may guide placement when appropriate.

After

Temporary soreness

Some stiffness or aching at the injection site is common for days afterward. Activity guidance varies by area treated.

Series

Multiple sessions

Prolotherapy is often done as a series spaced weeks apart. Progress depends on tissue quality, stability goals, and the broader plan.

Does prolotherapy hurt?

You may feel pressure or brief discomfort during injections. Soreness afterward is common and usually resolves within several days.

How many treatments will I need?

Some patients improve over a few sessions; others need a longer series. Your provider discusses a realistic plan based on your starting point.

Can I exercise after prolotherapy?

Activity restrictions vary by injection site and session. Your provider gives specific guidance — often a period of modified activity followed by gradual return to movement.

Is prolotherapy only for arthritis?

No. It may be considered for ligament laxity, instability, tendon or connective tissue patterns, and pain that keeps returning because the joint is not well supported.

How does it fit with PRP or stem cell therapies?

PRP, stem cell therapies, and exosomes can build on the same stability-and-repair concept. The choice depends on the tissue, the severity, and the overall plan.

How do I know if this belongs in my plan?

The starting point is the Get to Know You Consult. If IRM may be a fit, the next step may include assessment, orthopedic exam, ultrasound imaging when appropriate, and a personalized care discussion.
